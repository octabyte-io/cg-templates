The WhatsApp Business API, now Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform, is the official way for a business's software to send and receive WhatsApp messages: notifications, support chats and chatbots, with verified business profiles and approved message templates, sold by Meta directly or through providers such as Twilio.

Small businesses and developers look for an alternative because the official platform charges for conversations and messages, is set up through Meta's business tools, and asks for outgoing message templates to be approved. WAHA does the everyday part for free: it links your existing number by QR code, sends messages from your apps and passes incoming ones to them through webhooks. Evolution API adds ready-made chatbot and inbox integrations and can also use the official Cloud API, OpenWA adds message templates, auto-replies and API keys limited to chosen chats, and WuzAPI does the job in a smaller package for developers who write their own code. The catch is that the QR-code link is unofficial, so WhatsApp can ban the number, and it is only sensible where that risk is acceptable. GOWA, also free, does the same in a lighter program and adds scheduled sends and a way for AI agents to send messages, with the same risk. Waxum, another small program for developers, can also run a number on Meta's official Cloud API.
