### OJS and Scholastica pricing

As of November 2026, Scholastica sells three services, priced in dollars:

- **Peer review system**: $10 per submission and $500 a year. Journals with 500 or more submissions a year pay $8 per submission. Submissions are billed monthly, and the yearly fee once a year.
- **Open access publishing platform**: $1,549 a year per journal, or $1,239 per journal for publishers with 10 or more journals.
- **Production service (typesetting)**: $7 per 500 words, $7 per figure or table and $7 per block equation, billed monthly.

Scholastica lists no setup fees and no long-term contract. A journal can also have authors pay the submission fee.

Open Journal Systems has no licence fee and no paid edition. Your cost is the server: on Cloudgate, OJS runs on a small server. DOIs need your journal's own Crossref or DataCite membership, which applies whichever system you use.

### Peer review and editorial work

Both cover the path from submission to decision. In OJS, authors submit through a step-by-step form, editors invite reviewers, choose anonymous or open review, set due dates and let the journal send reminders. Each manuscript moves from review to copyediting and production on one dashboard, with statistics such as acceptance rates and days to a first decision.

Scholastica's peer review system has a manuscript table, task assignment and reminders, file versions and anonymization, email templates and analytics, and optional Similarity Check and RightsLink connections.

### The journal website and hosting

OJS includes the published journal: volumes and issues on a website that works on phones, with themes and PDF, HTML and other formats. One installation can host many journals in one or many languages.

Scholastica's website is a separate product, its open access publishing platform, priced per journal. It includes hosting and design, a website editor, a PDF viewer, readership analytics and archiving.

### Typesetting and indexing

Scholastica sells typesetting: layout, reference checking and proofing, with formatted PDFs and JATS XML. It also sends metadata to Crossref, DOAJ, PubMed Central and Portico.

OJS has no typesetting service; you upload finished files. It registers DOIs with Crossref or DataCite, links ORCID iDs and exports article details for Google Scholar, DOAJ and PubMed.

### Setup and support

Scholastica includes training and user support for everyone at no charge. With OJS, you set up the journal yourself, including its sections, review forms and emails, and add an email server so authors and reviewers are notified. Help comes from PKP's documentation and community forum, and PKP sells hosting, support and training for those who want it.
