### Piwigo and Flickr pricing

As of November 2026, Flickr has a free account and a paid Pro plan:

- **Free**: up to 1,000 photos and videos, with only 50 private.
- **Pro**: unlimited storage and private photos, no ads, detailed stats, 6K photo display, 10-minute videos, priority support and partner discounts. Flickr offers monthly, yearly and two-year plans, which renew automatically and are not refundable. Its Pro pages show no price before you start to sign up, so check the current price there.

Self-hosted Piwigo has no fee, no storage plan and no paid features. Your cost is the server and its disk. If you would rather not run a server, Piwigo's makers sell Piwigo Cloud from €32 a month, billed yearly and excluding VAT, for one administrator and 10 GB.

### Your own website or a community

This is the real difference. Flickr is a community: people follow you, fave and comment on your photos, post them in groups and may find them on Explore.

Piwigo is your own gallery. Visitors browse by album, tag or date, search, watch a slideshow, comment, keep favourites and download, but nothing brings new viewers to it. You share links to albums wherever your audience already is.

### Albums, privacy and organising

Both sort photos into albums with titles, tags and descriptions. Piwigo adds sub-albums and a Batch Manager that tags, moves, dates or deletes hundreds of photos at once. It reads the camera, date and keywords stored in each photo file.

For privacy, Piwigo makes each album public or visible only to chosen users and groups. Flickr has privacy controls on every photo, but a free account can keep only 50 photos private.

### Video, apps and extras

Flickr plays video, up to 10 minutes on Pro. Piwigo needs a plugin to play videos. Both have official phone apps: Flickr for iPhone and Android, and Piwigo for iPhone and Piwigo NG for Android, which connect to your own gallery for uploads.

Piwigo has a large catalogue of free plugins and themes, for maps, watermarks, videos and new looks. Its default themes look plainer than Flickr, so a new theme helps.

### Running it yourself

Flickr runs everything for you. With Piwigo you look after the server, its updates and its backups, and you add an email service if you want password resets and notifications.
