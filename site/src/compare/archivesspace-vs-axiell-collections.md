### ArchivesSpace and Axiell Collections pricing

As of November 2026, Axiell shows no public prices for Axiell Collections. Its product page asks you to request a quote for pricing information.

ArchivesSpace has no licence fee and no paid edition. An optional paid membership gives you the user manual, support and a say in its development, but every feature is in the free software. Your cost is the server: this template uses one with 4 GB of memory. Lyrasis, the organisation that leads ArchivesSpace, and other registered service providers also sell hosting.

### What each one manages

ArchivesSpace is made for archives. Staff record accessions, describe each collection as a tree of series, files and items, record condition assessments, track boxes and shelf locations, and keep one record for each person, organisation or subject. It describes digital material and links to files stored elsewhere, but it does not store them.

Axiell Collections manages museum and archive collections in one database, from archives to art and anthropology. Beyond cataloguing and accessions it handles loans, movements, storage, conservation, exhibition planning and events, and shows images through IIIF with deep zoom. Axiell sells digital asset management and barcode relocation as separate products.

### Standards, import and publishing

Both follow archival standards. ArchivesSpace uses DACS and imports or exports EAD, EAC-CPF, MARCXML, METS, MODS and Dublin Core, and loads accessions or folder lists from spreadsheets. Its public catalogue is built in: researchers search, cite and request published records and download finding aids as PDF.

Axiell Collections follows SPECTRUM, ISAD(G), DACS, RAD, MARC and CIDOC guidelines, imports CSV, exports XML, Excel and other formats, and has an open API. It publishes collections online through Axiell Internet Server or other web publishing software.

### Hosting

Axiell Collections runs in the browser, either in Axiell's cloud service or on your own servers with Microsoft SQL Server. ArchivesSpace also runs in the browser, in eleven languages, with separate staff and public addresses, on a server you choose. With ArchivesSpace you look after backups and upgrades yourself, or pay a hosting provider. Both let you set permissions for each user or group, so volunteers can see only what they should.
