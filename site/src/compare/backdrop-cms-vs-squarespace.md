### Backdrop CMS and Squarespace pricing

As of November 2026, Squarespace has four plans, shown in US dollars:

- **Basic**: $19 a month billed yearly ($228 a year) or $25 billed monthly, with 2 contributors and a 2% fee on online store sales.
- **Core**: $29 a month billed yearly ($348 a year) or $39 billed monthly, with unlimited contributors and no store transaction fee.
- **Plus**: $49 a month billed yearly ($588 a year) or $65 billed monthly.
- **Advanced**: $99 a month billed yearly ($1,188 a year) or $139 billed monthly.

Every plan starts with a 14-day free trial, and there is no free plan after it. Yearly plans include a free domain for the first year. Card payments cost 2.5% to 2.9% plus $0.30, and sales of digital content and memberships carry a fee of 7% on Basic, 5% on Core, 1% on Plus and none on Advanced.

Backdrop CMS has no licence fee, and its add-ons are free. Your cost is the server: Backdrop needs PHP 7.1 or later and MySQL or MariaDB, and on Cloudgate it runs on a small server.

### Hosting and ownership

Squarespace runs only on its own hosting. You pay the plan for as long as the site is online, and its export covers only part of a site.

Backdrop CMS runs on a server you choose, so your pages, user accounts and files stay with you and can be moved to another host. The trade-off is that you look after updates and backups, where Squarespace does that for you. Backdrop ships a new release about every four months.

### Editing and design

Squarespace starts from a designed template, and its Fluid Engine editor lets you drag blocks into place and resize them on a grid, with a separate mobile layout.

Backdrop CMS works differently. You write pages and news posts in a visual text editor, pick a layout, and place blocks such as menus, recent posts or a sidebar in its regions. There is no drag-and-drop builder, so there is more to learn, and the design comes from a theme. Backdrop has fewer themes than Squarespace has templates.

### Structured content, Views and roles

This is where Backdrop CMS is strongest. You can create your own content types, such as events, staff profiles or projects, each with its own fields, and use Views, built in, to list them as tables, lists or blocks, such as upcoming events on the home page. Roles decide exactly who can create, edit or publish each kind of content.

Backdrop is a fork of Drupal 7, kept up to date by its own community, so Drupal 7 sites can be upgraded to it with their content and accounts. It is not modern Drupal, and modules made for Drupal 10 or 11 do not run on it.

### What Squarespace has that Backdrop CMS lacks

- **A shop and memberships.** Every Squarespace plan can sell products, and digital content and memberships are built in. Backdrop CMS has no shop in its core.
- **Polished templates.** Squarespace sites look designed from the start; Backdrop needs a theme and some layout work.
- **A phone app.** The Squarespace app for iPhone and Android edits site copy, uploads photos and handles orders and payments. Backdrop has no app.
- **Managed hosting.** Squarespace looks after hosting and updates for the monthly fee.
