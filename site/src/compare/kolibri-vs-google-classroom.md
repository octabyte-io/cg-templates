### Where Kolibri and Google Classroom run

Kolibri is built for places with poor internet. It runs on a server or a local computer, and learners connect over the local network, with or without internet. Learners can take work home in the Kolibri Android app, which runs on Android 6 or newer and syncs when they return, and a Kolibri computer at a school can copy channels from your server and sync progress back.

Google Classroom is a Google cloud service. Learners and teachers sign in with Google accounts on the web or in the iPhone and Android apps.

### A library of lessons or a place to post work

Kolibri comes with content. You import free channels from the Kolibri Library, such as Khan Academy, CK-12, PhET simulations and TED-Ed, which Learning Equality says span more than 173 languages. Coaches put resources into lessons and quizzes for a class or a small group. Your own material goes in as a channel built in Kolibri Studio, not as loose files.

Google Classroom starts empty and holds what teachers post: assignments, materials and questions, made with Google Docs, Forms, Drive and hundreds of education apps.

### Assignments, grading and progress

This is where Google Classroom does more. Teachers assign, grade and give feedback across classes, with rubrics students can see, comment banks, bulk grading, custom grading periods, originality reports that check work for plagiarism, and grade export to student information systems such as PowerSchool and Infinite Campus. Gemini in Classroom adds no-cost AI tools for teachers.

Kolibri tracks progress on its library: who started or finished each resource, time spent, quiz scores and the questions learners found hardest, with reports to print or download. Learners cannot hand in files, and there is no essay grading.

### Kolibri and Google Classroom pricing

As of November 2026, Google Classroom comes with Google Workspace for Education Fundamentals, which Google offers free to qualifying schools. Education Plus adds security, analytics, AI tools and storage, and add-ons such as Google AI Pro for Education add more; Google shows "Contact for pricing" for these, sold as yearly subscriptions. Classroom is also open to people over 13 with a personal Google account, with lower class limits.

Kolibri is free and open source under the MIT licence, and its channels are free, each under its own content licence. You pay for the server: Learning Equality recommends 1 GB of memory on Linux, and disk space depends on the channels you import, since video channels are large. On Cloudgate, Kolibri gets a small server.

### Privacy and accounts

Kolibri keeps learner names and progress on your server, with no Google or other outside accounts. Google Classroom keeps data in Google Workspace; Google says there are no ads in Classroom and that it does not take ownership of user data in its core services.
