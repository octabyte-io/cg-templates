### webtrees and Ancestry pricing

As of November 2026, you can build a tree on Ancestry for free: add as many people as you like, get Ancestry Hints, see record previews and upload photos and documents. A membership opens full access to its historical records, including original document images. Ancestry's pages show:

- **U.S. Discovery**: from $24.99 a month.
- **World Explorer**: from $39.99 a month.
- **All Access**: the top membership, at a higher price.

Prices are in US dollars on Ancestry's US site. AncestryDNA tests are sold separately. If a membership ends, your account stays as a free registered guest and you can still edit your tree.

webtrees costs nothing: its site says it "costs $0" and takes optional donations. Your cost is the server.

### Records, hints and research

This is the main difference. Ancestry's value is its billions of historical records and the hints that suggest them for people in your tree.

webtrees has no records of its own; you bring your research. It gives you a place to keep it well: sources, notes, photos, certificates and stories attached to people and events, with a calendar of anniversaries and statistics about your tree.

### A website you control

webtrees turns your tree into a family history website. You can open it to visitors or keep it for signed-in relatives. Privacy can be set for the whole site, each tree, each user, each person or a single fact, and living people show as "Private" to visitors. Relatives get accounts as members, editors or moderators, and new edits can wait for a moderator's approval.

Ancestry keeps your tree on its own service, where you invite family and friends to view it. With webtrees, you look after the server and its backups.

### Charts, reports and languages

webtrees has pedigree, descendant, fan, hourglass, interactive, timeline and relationship charts, and PDF reports such as family group sheets, Ahnentafel and descendant reports. Maps show where people were born, married and lived. It comes in over 60 languages, with naming customs such as surname first or patronymic surnames, and six themes.

### What Ancestry has that webtrees lacks

- **Record collections and hints.** Ancestry's memberships open billions of historical records.
- **DNA tests and matches.** Ancestry sells AncestryDNA kits; webtrees has no DNA tools.
- **Phone apps.** Ancestry has apps for iPhone, Android and Amazon devices. webtrees works in a phone's browser.
- **A managed service.** Ancestry runs everything; you don't need a server.
