### calibre and Kindle pricing

As of November 2026, Kindle costs what you spend on books and devices:

- **Kindle apps**: free for iPhone, Android, PC and Mac, and Kindle for Web in the browser.
- **Kindle store**: you buy each book.
- **Kindle+** (formerly Kindle Unlimited): $11.99 a month plus taxes in the US, with millions of e-books, comics, manga and magazines, and audio narration for thousands of titles. You can borrow up to 20 books at a time and lose access to them when the subscription ends.
- **Kindle e-readers**: Amazon lists the Kindle from $149.99, Kindle Paperwhite from $199.99 and Kindle Colorsoft from $289.99 in the US.

calibre has no price and no paid edition. Because the whole program runs on the server, it needs a medium server with about 2 GB of memory, plus disk space for your library.

### A file manager, not a store

Kindle is built around buying and reading. Kindle-store books are copy-protected and open only in Kindle apps and devices, and since February 26, 2025, Amazon no longer offers "Download & Transfer via USB" for them.

calibre is built around files you own. It sorts and searches your library by title, author, series, tags or your own fields, downloads book details and covers, and can turn articles from news sites or any RSS feed into an e-book. It has no store, and it does not remove copy protection.

### Converting and editing

This is what calibre does that Kindle doesn't. It converts between dozens of formats, such as EPUB, MOBI, AZW3 and PDF, and its editor changes the text, styles and table of contents of EPUB and AZW3 books with a live preview. An author can check a finished EPUB and make a Kindle copy in the same place.

Amazon's Send to Kindle takes EPUB, PDF, DOCX, TXT, RTF, HTML and image files, but calibre's FAQ notes that Amazon no longer accepts MOBI or AZW3 files by email, so EPUB is the format to send.

### Reading on your devices

Kindle reads well everywhere: Kindle e-readers, the Kindle apps and Kindle for Web, with notes, highlights, search and a dictionary.

calibre has a viewer with bookmarks, highlights and search, but it is a desktop program with menus and windows. In a browser on a laptop it works well; on a phone it is usable but cramped. It has one shared login, so it suits one person rather than a household.
