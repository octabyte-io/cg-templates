faq:
  - q: Is Writebook free?
    a: Yes, Writebook is free and open source under the MIT licence, made by 37signals, the company behind Basecamp. There is no paid edition; you pay only for your server.
  - q: Is Writebook a good alternative to GitBook?
    a: Yes, if you want to publish a manual, handbook or guide as a clean online book without a subscription. Writebook is much simpler than GitBook, with no Git sync, integrations or AI features, so a team that needs those may prefer a fuller docs tool.
  - q: How do I sign in to Writebook the first time?
    a: The first person to open your Writebook web address creates the admin account with a name, email and password; there is no default password. Everyone else joins through an invite link the admin shares.
  - q: Can I keep some books private?
    a: Yes, every Writebook book starts private and only people you invite can read it. You can publish any book to the web with one switch and keep others private on the same site.
  - q: Can Writebook export a book as a PDF or EPUB?
    a: No, Writebook publishes books as web pages only, with no PDF, EPUB or Kindle export. You can copy any page's Markdown out if you need it elsewhere.
  - q: Can I import my GitBook or Notion pages into Writebook?
    a: No, Writebook has no importer. Pages are written in Markdown, so you can export Markdown from your old tool and paste it into Writebook page by page.

    a: A small server is enough, because Writebook is one light app that keeps everything in a single built-in database. 37signals asks for at least 2 GB of memory and one processor.

Self-hosted Writebook is a free, open-source app from 37signals for publishing books, manuals and handbooks on the web, and a simple alternative to GitBook and Notion's public pages. You write each page in Markdown, add picture pages and section pages, give the book a cover, and publish it with one switch or keep it private for the people you invite.

A bike shop, for example, could keep a private staff handbook on opening up, taking repair bookings and closing the till, and publish a public cycling guide for customers on the same site. Teachers can share course notes, a software company can publish its product manual, and a family can collect its stories and photos in one book.

Books read well on a computer, tablet or phone, with search inside each book and full-screen reading. There is no phone app, and no PDF or EPUB export: Writebook publishes web books only.

Cloudgate installs Writebook on a server Cloudgate provides or one you connect, and gives it a secure web address. The first person to open it creates the admin account, then invites co-authors and readers with a link.
