faq:
  - q: Is Trilium Notes free?
    a: Yes. Trilium Notes is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence, with no paid edition and nothing behind a paywall. The optional AI chat uses an AI provider you choose and pay for yourself.
  - q: Is Trilium a good alternative to Evernote?
    a: Yes, for your own notes. Trilium imports Evernote's ENEX export files, keeps notes in a tree of any depth, and has a web clipper for saving pages from your browser. It does not read text from scanned documents and has no official phone app.
  - q: Is Trilium a good alternative to Obsidian?
    a: Yes, if you want your notes on your own server, open in any browser and synced to the desktop app at no extra cost. Obsidian keeps Markdown files on your device and has official phone apps and a much larger plugin library, while Trilium keeps notes in a database.
  - q: How do I sign in to Trilium the first time?
    a: Open Trilium's web address right after setup, choose to start a new knowledge base or sync from an existing Trilium, then set a password. There is no username, and whoever opens the address first sets that password, so do it straight away.
  - q: Can more than one person use Trilium?
    a: No, Trilium is built for one person, with a single password and no user accounts. For notes a group shares, a wiki or Memos, which has separate accounts, suits better.
  - q: Is there a Trilium phone app?
    a: No, there is no official phone app. Trilium has a touch-friendly mobile version in the browser that you can add to your home screen, and community-made apps exist for Android and iPhone.
  - q: Is Trilium the same as TriliumNext?
    a: Yes. TriliumNext is the community project that now maintains Trilium Notes, after its original developer handed over the project, and this template installs its current release. Notes from the original Trilium can be moved to it.

Trilium Notes is a free, open-source, self-hosted note-taking app for building a personal knowledge base, and an alternative to Evernote, Obsidian and, for one person's notes, Notion. You keep your notes in a tree as deep as you like, link them together, and search everything from any web browser.

A note can be rich text with tables and images, a code snippet, a drawing or a mind map, and a set of notes can show as a board, a table or a calendar. Trilium keeps earlier versions of each note and can lock sensitive ones with a password. A freelance translator, for example, could keep a glossary for each client, notes on every past job and reference articles in one tree, and find any term fast.

The Trilium desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux syncs with your server, so your notes are on your computer offline and in the browser everywhere else. On a phone, Trilium has a mobile version in the browser. You can import Evernote notebooks and Markdown files, and publish chosen notes as public web pages.

Cloudgate installs Trilium on a server Cloudgate provides or one you connect and gives it a secure web address. Open that address right after setup to set your password. Trilium is for one person, with no team accounts.
