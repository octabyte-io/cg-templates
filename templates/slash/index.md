Slash is a free, open-source, self-hosted go links app and an alternative to GoLinks: it gives the links your team uses every day short, memorable names such as s/handbook, on your own domain. Each link can carry tags and a description, be private, shared with your team or public, and show how often it was opened.

It suits a small team that keeps hunting for the same links in chat and email. A cleaning company, for example, could set up s/rota for the weekly schedule, s/timesheet for the hours form and s/supplies for the order page, then group them into a collection called new-starters and send that one address to every new hire. Nobody has to remember the long addresses.

The free version has no limit on links but allows up to five accounts and five shared collections; a paid Pro licence key from its makers removes those limits. Browser extensions for Chrome, Edge and Firefox let people type s/ and a name straight into the address bar, and there is no phone app.

Cloudgate installs Slash on a server Cloudgate provides or one you connect and gives it a secure web address, which is also the domain your short links use. It needs very little memory, so it fits the smallest server, and your links stay on your server.
