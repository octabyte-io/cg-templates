faq:
  - q: Is Kutt free?
    a: Yes, Kutt is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no limit on links or domains when you run it yourself. You pay only for your server. The Premium plans on kutt.to are for the project's own hosted service, not the software.
  - q: Is Kutt a good alternative to Bitly?
    a: Yes, if you want short links on your own domain with click stats and no monthly limits. Kutt covers shortening, branded domains, QR codes, passwords, expiry dates and per-link stats, but it has fewer marketing extras than Bitly's paid plans, such as campaigns and link-in-bio pages.
  - q: How do I use my own short domain with Kutt?
    a: Choose that domain, for example go.yourshop.com, as Kutt's web address when you set it up, and point the domain at your server in your domain provider's settings; Cloudgate shows you the record to add. Every short link then uses that domain. To add another domain later, point it at the server too, add it to the app in Cloudgate, then add it in Kutt's Settings.
  - q: How do I sign in to Kutt the first time?
    a: The first person to open Kutt's address creates the admin account with an email and password. Open it as soon as setup finishes, before you share the address. Public sign-up is off, so the admin adds other users.
  - q: Can I import my links from Bitly into Kutt?

Kutt is a free, open-source, self-hosted link shortener and an alternative to Bitly: it turns long web addresses into short links on your own domain and shows how many people clicked each one. You choose the ending of each link yourself, so a link reads go.yourshop.com/autumn instead of a jumble of letters.

It suits small businesses, marketers and creators who put links on printed material, social profiles and newsletters. A bakery, for example, could print a short link such as go.yourbakery.com/xmas and its QR code on a Christmas flyer, then see how many people opened the pre-order page, from which countries and whether on iPhone or Android. If the page moves, the bakery changes where the link points and the printed flyers keep working.

Kutt runs in your web browser and has browser extensions for Chrome and Firefox to shorten the page you're on. Links can have a password or an expiry date, and the admin decides who else gets an account.

Cloudgate installs Kutt on a server Cloudgate provides or one you connect and gives it a secure web address, which is also the domain your short links use, so pick a short one. Open it right away to create your admin account. Your links and click stats stay on your server.
