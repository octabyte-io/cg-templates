faq:
  - q: Is Drawnix free?
    a: Yes, Drawnix is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no paid edition and no fee per person. You pay only for your server.
  - q: Is Drawnix a good alternative to XMind?
    a: Yes, for making clear mind maps and saving them as pictures, in a browser and for free. It has fewer mind-map features than XMind, with no outline view or presentation mode, and it cannot open XMind files.
  - q: Is Drawnix a good alternative to Miro?
    a: Partly. Drawnix gives you an endless board with mind maps, flowcharts, shapes, drawing and images, but only one person works on a board at a time, with no live collaboration, comments or templates. If you need to draw together live, a shared whiteboard such as WBO fits better.
  - q: Where does Drawnix save my boards?
    a: In your own web browser. Drawnix saves the board automatically on the device you are using, not on the server, so it is private to you. Use Save File to keep a .drawnix copy, which is also how you back up a board, move it to another computer or keep several boards.
  - q: Can several people work on the same board?
    a: No, Drawnix has no accounts or live collaboration. To share a board, send the .drawnix file, which the other person opens in their own Drawnix, or export the board as a picture.
  - q: How do I sign in to Drawnix the first time?
    a: You don't; Drawnix has no sign-in. Open its web address and start drawing. The first time, the menus may show in Chinese, so open the menu at the top left, choose Language and pick English.
  - q: Can I import my mind maps from XMind or my boards from Miro?
    a: No, Drawnix cannot import XMind, Miro or Whimsical files. It opens .drawnix files and builds new mind maps from a Markdown outline and new flowcharts from Mermaid code.

Drawnix is a free, open-source whiteboard for mind maps, flowcharts and freehand drawing, and self-hosted Drawnix is a simple alternative to XMind and Whimsical. It runs in your web browser, and each board is saved on your own device as you work, with no account to create.

One endless board holds a mind map with coloured branches, flowcharts of shapes and arrows, freehand sketches, text and images. You can also start from text: paste a Markdown outline and it becomes a mind map, or paste Mermaid code and it becomes a flowchart you can keep editing. A bike repair shop, for example, could map out its spring open day as a mind map and sketch its repair process as a flowchart beside it, then export both as pictures for the staff noticeboard.

Drawnix is made for one person at a time. It has no live collaboration, comments or sign-in, and each browser keeps a single board, so save a .drawnix file to keep a copy, move a board to another computer or share it. If your team needs to draw together live, it is not a full Miro replacement.
