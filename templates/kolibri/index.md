faq:
  - q: Is Kolibri free?
    a: Yes, Kolibri is free and open source under the MIT licence, made by the non-profit Learning Equality. You pay only for your server. The learning channels are free too, though each comes with its own content licence.
  - q: Does Kolibri need the internet?
    a: No, Kolibri itself can run on a local network with no internet at all. When Cloudgate runs it on an online server, learners reach it over the internet, and the Kolibri Android app or a Kolibri computer at a school can copy channels from it, work offline and sync progress back later.
  - q: Is Kolibri a good alternative to Google Classroom?
    a: Yes, if your learners need lessons, quizzes and progress tracking that keep working without reliable internet. Kolibri comes with a ready library of learning material, but it does not collect homework files or link to Google Docs and Drive as Google Classroom does.
  - q: Can Kolibri show Khan Academy lessons offline?
    a: Yes, the Kolibri Library has Khan Academy channels in many languages, with videos and exercises you can import once and use offline. Kolibri is independent of Khan Academy, and your Khan Academy accounts and progress do not carry over.
  - q: How do I sign in to Kolibri the first time?
    a: There is no default password. The first time you open Kolibri's web address, a setup wizard asks how you will use it and has you create the super admin account. Keep that password safe, because it cannot be recovered if lost.
  - q: Can I add my own teaching material to Kolibri?
    a: Yes, but not by uploading files straight into Kolibri. You build a channel of your own material in Kolibri Studio, Learning Equality's free online tool, and then import that channel into your Kolibri.
  - q: What server size do I need for Kolibri?
    a: A small server is enough, since Learning Equality recommends 1 GB of memory on Linux. Disk space is the real limit, because it depends on how many channels you import, and video channels are large.

Kolibri is a free, open-source, offline-first learning platform you can self-host: learners open a library of lessons, videos, exercises and books in a browser, and teachers follow their progress. Made by the non-profit Learning Equality for places with poor internet, it can stand in for Google Classroom and bring Khan Academy lessons to learners on your own server.

You fill the library with free channels from the Kolibri Library, such as Khan Academy, CK-12, PhET simulations and TED-Ed, in many languages. Coaches put resources into lessons and quizzes for a class or a small group, then see who started, who finished and which questions were hard. A small after-school club, for example, could import a maths channel, give each child a login and set a weekly lesson, then see on Friday who finished it and which questions tripped them up.

Learners use any web browser or the Kolibri Android app, which works offline and syncs later. A Kolibri computer at a school can copy channels from your server and sync progress back. Cloudgate installs Kolibri on a server Cloudgate provides or one you connect, with a secure web address. Follow the short setup wizard to create your admin account, then import your first channels. Learner names and progress stay on your server.
