faq:
  - q: Is WooCommerce free?
    a: Yes, WooCommerce is free and open source under the GPL licence, and so is WordPress, so self-hosting it costs only your server. Some features are paid extensions, such as subscriptions, bookings and memberships, and premium themes cost extra.
  - q: Does WooCommerce take a cut of each sale?
    a: No, WooCommerce charges no fee per sale and no monthly platform fee. The payment provider you connect, such as Stripe or PayPal, still charges its own card fees on each payment.
  - q: Is WooCommerce a good alternative to Shopify?
    a: Yes, if you want to own your store and avoid a monthly plan and per-sale platform fees. In return you look after updates, backups and security yourself, and some features that come with a Shopify plan are paid extensions in WooCommerce.
  - q: Can I move my Shopify store to WooCommerce?
    a: Partly. WooCommerce imports products from a spreadsheet (CSV) out of the box. Moving customers, orders and pages from Shopify needs an extension from the WooCommerce Marketplace, such as a paid Shopify migration tool.
  - q: How do I set up my store the first time?
    a: Open your store's web address straight after Cloudgate sets it up and run WordPress's short installer, which creates your admin account; whoever does it first becomes the admin. WooCommerce is already installed and switches on by itself, then its setup wizard asks for your store address and what you sell.
  - q: Will my store send order emails to customers?
    a: Yes, once you connect an email sending service. Order confirmations and other store emails need a sending service such as your email provider's SMTP settings, added through an email plugin.
  - q: What server size do I need for WooCommerce?
    a: A server with 2 GB of memory, which is what Cloudgate asks for. A store needs more room than a simple website, and you can move to a bigger server if your shop gets busy or you add many plugins.

WooCommerce is a free, open-source plugin that turns WordPress into a self-hosted online store, an alternative to Shopify, Wix and Squarespace where you sell from your own website and keep your store's data. You get a product catalogue, cart and checkout, orders, stock, discount codes, shipping and tax, all managed from the WordPress dashboard.

It suits small shops selling physical goods, digital downloads or services. A pottery studio, for example, could list its mugs and bowls with photos and prices, put a few on sale, charge one shipping rate for the UK and another for Europe, and pack each order from the orders list, paying no monthly platform fee and no cut of each sale to WooCommerce.

Payments come through providers such as Stripe and PayPal, whose plugins are free; they charge their usual card fees. Paid extensions add subscriptions, bookings and memberships, and the free WooCommerce app for iPhone and Android lets you check orders on the go.

Cloudgate installs WordPress with WooCommerce ready on a server Cloudgate provides or one you connect, and gives it a secure web address. Open it right away to create your admin account; WooCommerce then switches on and its setup wizard walks you through your store details. Connect an email sending service so customers get their order emails.
